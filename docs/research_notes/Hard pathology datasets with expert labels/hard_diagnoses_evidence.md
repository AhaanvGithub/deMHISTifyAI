# Hard histopathology diagnoses: inter/intra-observer agreement, clinical stakes, and criteria changes (research-to-practice gap)

Notes compiled 2026-10-18. Sources were retrieved by web search and abstract/page fetch; full texts were mostly not read, so figures come from abstracts or publisher/repository summaries. Items marked **[DOI from prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session]** are well-known landmark papers or guidelines whose content I am confident of but did not re-open; the report writer should treat them as lower-verification.

Kappa interpretation (Landis & Koch): <0.20 slight, 0.21-0.40 fair, 0.41-0.60 moderate, 0.61-0.80 substantial, >0.80 almost perfect.

## Q1. Best-cited agreement numbers for each distinction, and who measured them

### Takeaway
Nearly every distinction on the list lands in the "fair to moderate" kappa band (about 0.2-0.6), and the worst agreement is always in the *middle* categories: indefinite/low-grade dysplasia, atypia, CIN2, moderately dysplastic nevi, SSL with dysplasia. The best-measured (large-panel) examples are breast atypia (115 pathologists, JAMA 2015) and melanocytic lesions (187 pathologists, BMJ 2017).

### Inferences
- The "hard middle" pattern is consistent: categories that sit at a management threshold (LGD, atypia, CIN2, MPATH-Dx II-IV, SSL-D, IND) are both least reproducible and most consequential. These are natural targets for an "expert-labelled hard cases" dataset.
- Reported kappas are inflated by calibrated expert panels (e.g., OED 0.75-0.78 among calibrated pathologists vs 0.49 against routine service). Community-practice agreement is likely lower than most published numbers.
- Providing clinical context (polyp size/site) raises SSL agreement from 0.46 to 0.63, suggesting tile-only labels for SSL are intrinsically noisier than whole-case labels.

### Gaps
- Exact Vennalaganti 2017 kappa values (PDF fetch returned binary); commonly cited as very low (~0.1-0.2) for LGD/IND but not verified here.
- Montgomery et al. 2001 (Hum Pathol 32:368) Barrett's kappas not retrieved this session.
- No study found reporting a kappa specifically for Huvos necrosis grading or percent-necrosis estimation.
- No published B-Path kappa (Elmore reports percent concordance); classic ADH studies (Rosai 1991, Schnitt 1992) not retrieved.
- Allsbrook 2001 general-pathologist study (41 pathologists) not retrieved; commonly cited overall kappa ~0.435 is unverified here.
- CIN-specific (CIN2 alone) kappa from ALTS not available in abstract.

## Q2. Which have the biggest clinical consequence of disagreement?

### Takeaway
Highest stakes: Barrett's LGD (ablation vs surveillance), melanocytic class III-IV (wide excision/sentinel node vs nothing), breast atypia vs DCIS (surgery ± radiation vs surveillance), CIN1 vs CIN2 (excision with obstetric risk vs observation), prostate GG1 vs GG2/cribriform (active surveillance vs radical treatment), and NIFTP vs PTC (lobectomy only vs completion thyroidectomy + RAI). SSL vs HP mostly affects surveillance intervals.

### Cited Findings
- Barrett's: consensus LGD (≥2 GI pathologists agreeing) significantly associated with progression, i.e. many community LGD diagnoses are over-calls — [PubMed 11151865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11151865/)
- Melanocytic: misclassification spans moderately dysplastic nevi to early invasive melanoma, the range where re-excision and staging decisions are made — [PubMed 28659278](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28659278/)
- Breast: atypia under-interpretation 35%; atypia drives excision and chemoprevention decisions; DCIS concordance 84% with 13% under-interpretation — [CLP mag summary](https://clpmag.com/resource-center/research/study-examines-diagnostic-accuracy-pathologists-interpreting-breast-biopsies/); [MedicalResearch.com](https://medicalresearch.com/pathologists-dont-always-agree-on-breast-biopsy-atypia-or-dcis/)
- CIN: dichotomising by management (CIN2+ vs <CIN2) with p16 reaches κ 0.84 — the management threshold is where p16 is most useful — [AJCP 2024](https://researchexchange.ocrahope.org/papers/ajcp-aqae029)
- NIFTP: scoring systems failed to separate NIFTP from EFVPTC with nodal metastasis or BRAF V600E, which is the risk of under-treatment from reclassification — [PMC8098764](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8098764)
- Gastric: OLGIM staging (most reproducible) misses a substantial proportion of potentially high-risk individuals; reproducibility and risk-capture trade off — [Virchows Arch 2014](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s00428-014-1544-3)
- Osteosarcoma: poor responders (Huvos I-II) have inferior 5-year survival; ≥90% necrosis associated with DFS in Greek real-world data — [Cancer Diagn Progn](https://www.cancerdiagnosisprognosis.org/article/552/real-world-data-from-patients-with-osteosarcoma-treated-in-8-medical-centers-in-greece-ninety-percent-or-greater-tumor-necrosis-is-associated-with-disease-free-survival)

### Inferences (consequence ranking; author judgement from the above plus standard guideline knowledge)
1. Barrett's LGD vs NDBE/IND — endoscopic ablation vs 3-5 yr surveillance; IND triggers repeat endoscopy.
2. Melanocytic MPATH-Dx III/IV vs II — wide re-excision, SLNB, oncology follow-up vs none (over- and under-treatment).
3. Breast ADH vs DCIS vs UDH — surgical excision, radiation, endocrine therapy vs surveillance.
4. Prostate GG1 vs GG2 and presence of cribriform — eligibility for active surveillance.
5. CIN1 vs CIN2/3 — LEEP (preterm birth risk) vs observation.
6. NIFTP vs invasive EFVPTC — extent of surgery, RAI.
7. IBD dysplasia (indefinite/LGD) — colectomy vs intensified surveillance.
8. Osteosarcoma necrosis — prognostic; historically chemo modification (EURAMOS-1 showed no benefit of changing post-op chemo, so consequence now chiefly prognostic) **[not sourced this session]**.
9. Oral dysplasia grade, gastric dysplasia/IM, tumour budding (adjuvant chemo in stage II CRC, T1 resection decisions), lung IASLC grade (prognostic), breast grade (adjuvant therapy), SSL vs HP (surveillance interval 3 vs 5-10 years).

### Gaps
- No study found that directly quantifies downstream harm (e.g., unnecessary procedures) per misclassification for most entities except via concordance numbers.

## Q3. Which are visible on H&E tiles vs requiring IHC or whole-slide context?

### Takeaway
Most are H&E-native, but several depend on context beyond a single tile: SSL vs HP benefits from size/site; NIFTP requires evaluation of the entire capsule (invasion exclusion); osteosarcoma necrosis is a whole-resection map; tumour budding uses a hotspot across the invasive front; predominant-pattern grading needs whole-tumour percentages. CIN2 is aided by p16 IHC.

### Cited Findings
- SSL agreement rises with size/site (κ 0.46 -> 0.63) — [Histopathology 2026](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/despite-simplified-diagnostic-criteria-intraobserver-and-interobs/)
- CIN2+ kappa 0.41 on H&E vs 0.73 with p16 — [PubMed 25389329](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25389329/)
- Tumour budding: H&E ICC 0.58 vs pan-CK 0.83 in 2015; 2024 study found H&E reproducible (κ 0.81) and IHC only useful in specific situations — [RERO](https://doc.rero.ch/record/331644/files/428_2015_Article_1740.pdf); [Surg Exp Pathol 2024](https://biomedcentral-surgexppathol.publicaciones.saludcastillayleon.es/counter/pdf/10.1186/s42047-024-00144-6.pdf)
- Lung pattern variability significantly higher in cases with more slides (whole-tumour burden) — [Heidelberg record](https://heibib.ub.uni-heidelberg.de/search/Record/1588362434)
- Breast grading less reproducible on WSI (60%) than glass (68%); pleomorphism worst — [DTIC](https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/AD1100232.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Tile-friendly (H&E, local morphology):** SSL vs HP crypt architecture (but noisy), Barrett's dysplasia, IBD dysplasia, CIN grade (H&E; p16 adjunct), OED grade, gastric IM/dysplasia, Gleason 3 vs 4 / cribriform, nuclear pleomorphism, mitotic figures, NIFTP nuclear score, melanocytic cytology.
- **Requires whole-slide or case context:** NIFTP (capsule/invasion, ≥? sampling), melanoma vs nevus (architecture, symmetry, depth, maturation), ADH (size/extent ≤2 mm or ≤2 spaces criterion), lung IASLC grade (% patterns, ≥20% high-grade), Gleason score/GG (% pattern 4), tumour budding hotspot, osteosarcoma % necrosis mapping, Barrett's IND (inflammation context, surface maturation across biopsy), SSL size/site.
- **IHC-assisted:** CIN2 (p16), tumour budding (pan-CK optional), Barrett's (p53 adjunct per recent guidelines) **[p53 not sourced this session]**.

### Gaps
- No systematic source compared tile-level vs WSI-level agreement for these entities.

## Q4. Where have criteria changed in the last ~7 years such that practice may lag?

### Takeaway
Major criteria shifts 2016-2022: WHO 2019 digestive (SSA/P renamed SSL; one unequivocal distorted crypt suffices; SSL-D), ITBCC 2016 budding (hotspot, 0.785 mm², 3-tier), NIFTP 2016 (reclassification of EFVPTC), ISUP 2019/GUPS 2020 (diverging rules on tertiary/minor pattern, cribriform reporting), IASLC 2020 lung grading (predominant + ≥20% high-grade), WHO 2022 thyroid and 5th-edition breast/skin/female genital. Agreement studies done under the new criteria (e.g., SSL under WHO 2019) show agreement did not improve.

### Cited Findings
- WHO 2019 (5th ed) SSL criteria applied in 2026 multicentre study still gave SSL Fleiss κ 0.30-0.44 and SSL-D 0.11-0.13; accuracy for SSL 58-62% — [WJG 2026](https://www.wjgnet.com/1007-9327/full/v32/i42/120411.htm)
- Title of 2026 Histopathology paper: "Despite simplified diagnostic criteria, intraobserver and interobserver variability remain..." — [WUSTL](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/despite-simplified-diagnostic-criteria-intraobserver-and-interobs/)
- Systematic review covering WHO 2000/2010/2019 criteria: reproducibility very poor to moderate across editions — [Thieme](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/pdf/10.1055/s-0041-1730016.pdf)
- NIFTP refinement (Alves 2018): nuclear score 2-3 required plus exclusion criteria (no papillae, no psammoma bodies etc.) — [Springer Medicine](https://www.springermedicine.com/non-invasive-follicular-thyroid-neoplasm-with-papillary-like-nuc/22032738)
- Riddell 1983 grading still guides IBD management; Harpaz 2023 consensus adds subtypes (intestinal vs gastric, serrated) — [Mount Sinai](https://scholars.mssm.edu/en/publications/colorectal-dysplasia-in-chronic-inflammatory-bowel-disease-a-cont/)

### Inferences
- Practice-lag hotspots: (a) SSL criteria — new single-crypt threshold has not improved agreement, so labels in older archives (pre-2019 "HP") likely contain unrecognized SSLs; (b) prostate — ISUP vs GUPS disagree on handling minor high-grade patterns and intraductal carcinoma, so labels depend on which convention the lab follows; cribriform reporting is recommended but inconsistently done; (c) lung — IASLC 2020 grade requires estimating high-grade patterns ≥20%, adding a threshold to an already moderate-agreement task; (d) thyroid — pre-2016 FVPTC archives need reclassification; (e) melanocytic — MPATH-Dx v2 (2023) collapsed categories, older datasets used 5-class v1; (f) cervical — LAST/WHO move to LSIL/HSIL with p16 for CIN2 means CIN1/2/3-labelled datasets don't map cleanly.
- For dataset construction, criteria edition must be recorded per label.

### Gaps
